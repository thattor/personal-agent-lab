## 1. Substantiated or possible defect?

**Neither addition is a substantiated defect against the named-case requirements.** The action and routing are correct:

- **Input 1:** `none` plus an explanation is right. Sending is unsupported, and the prompt forbids a silent partial compound request.
- **Input 2:** `local_draft` is right. It cites the prior turn's record, which establishes 架空 and 田辺さん, and the current record.
- **The spec guards against fabrication:** it explicitly forbids inventing content, results or plans.

**「送付された資料」 is a possible minor grounding drift, not an observed defect.**

- The user said only 資料を確認した. "Sent by Tanabe" is a premise about the counterparty's action, which is closer to D029's "never invent… facts" than to a harmless format choice.
- It is also the overwhelmingly natural reading of a confirm-receipt note, and Tanabe is fictional. Under D029's "infer harmless reversible details" and P002's "avoid unnecessary requirements", a reasonable reviewer could accept it.
- The expert artifact was not run. Whether 「お送りいただいた」 appears, and whether it matters, is unobserved.

**Classification: possible, low severity.**

**The 「［署名］」 placeholder is a format decision, not a grounding defect.**

- It introduces no fact.
- It is the spec's way of honouring "do not invent the sender name", which is consistent with D029.
- Host template-eligibility being false only means it is not protected as an explicit blank template. It does not make the placeholder a violation.
- Treating it as a failure would be style policing.

**Verdict on the stop:** halting on these grounds was controller overreach unless the frozen criteria already named premise additions. Do not retrofit a criterion now.

## 2. Prompt delta: optional, small, general

A change is justifiable only as a clarification of the existing D029 sentence, not as a fix for an observed failure. If adopted, append after "Infer harmless reversible details; never invent personal facts, decisions, dates or commitments.":

> In a draft spec, format choices (subject, greeting, closing, length, sign-off style) are yours to make. Do not add premises about what happened or who did what, or content the request and context do not support; when such a detail is unknown, keep the user's own neutral wording. Treat unknown optional items as format the drafter may omit, not as required content; keep placeholders the user explicitly requests.

Why this shape:

- **No hardcoded names, phrases or regex.** It separates format freedom from premise addition, which is the actual distinction at issue.
- **Generic and fictional drafts are unaffected.** They still proceed without questions, because the delta restricts only premises, not inference of tone or structure.
- **Explicit blank templates are unaffected.** Their placeholders remain mandatory.
- **It does not ban placeholders.** It only stops the Primary from making them a requirement, which avoids new trivial judgments.

**Risk:** over-literal compliance producing stilted specs. That is why it says "keep the user's own neutral wording" rather than "omit".

**Doing nothing is also defensible.** The drafter's system prompt already says to omit unprovided details. This would be a second guard at the spec layer, not a sole fix.

## 3. Protections the delta must not disturb

- **Same-Goal correction:** `control/correct` must still emit a complete corrected spec that carries forward all user-supplied facts from earlier turns. "Keep the user's own wording" must not be read as "drop earlier context".
- **Source IDs:** multi-turn grounding is correct here. Including `af98…` is required, since it supplies 架空 and the recipient. The delta must not imply citing only the current input.
- **Unchanged behaviour:** the `none` on Input 1, no claims of sending, and the host's ownership of effects and template eligibility all stay as they are.
